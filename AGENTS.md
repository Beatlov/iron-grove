# Project instructions

Desktop-mouse, single-player military space pixel strategy. User-facing text is Russian. Humans versus technological demons in space armor, on ruined industrial planets. Keep the H3 olive/sand humans, graphite/red demons, bone trim, green/amber indicators and coarse native pixel clusters.

- Offline operation and file:// support; no build tools, dependencies, remote assets/fonts or telemetry. Preview: `python3 serve.py 8000` from any cwd. Keep HTML asset query versions and visible HELIOS build stamp in sync.
- Simulation in engine.js is DOM-independent, exported through window.RTS and CommonJS. art.js must never mutate simulation. UI and scenes belong in game.js.
- H4 has 12 space regions, each a separate planet surface of 144×96 tiles. Local maps have no regional subdivision or capture outposts. Only owned surfaces may be opened; foreign planets expose status and expedition targets.
- Food, money, metal, crystals, gas and weapons physically occupy inventories of bases (5000 shared capacity) and warehouses (500). Team stock is an aggregate view over ready stores, excluding reserved weapons, never an independent duplicate ledger. Costs and upkeep use the shared total; weapon orders reserve real local-store inventory until pickup. Carriers must retain loads when local stores are full. Captures transfer inventories; destroyed stores lose goods. Population is global: residents + military/training + six reserved places per colony mission. Do not duplicate counts when units travel or enter battle.
- Residents are humans, never manually commanded, automatically build/repair/transport. Hire them for 20 money at a base in a sequential 8-second queue (max 5), reserving population capacity immediately. Staffing is controlled by building cards. Stationary workers hide indoors; farmers sow fields (0.6 worker-seconds), grow crops (6 seconds), harvest exactly 4 per cycle (0.6 seconds) and process for 0.4 seconds. No new sowing until growth and all harvesting are finished; early harvest must preserve the fixed cycle yield. No automatic births or converting residents into soldiers.
- Gas is collected at finite sources within 9 tiles of a gas station (2 assigned collectors), returned and sealed there, then transported as ordinary goods. Raw loads never enter storage directly. Ore and gas are conditionally tier 1 and weapons tier 2; tiers are metadata only, without special visuals or logistics. Weapons recipe: 8 metal + 3 crystals + 2 gas.
- Military units exist in campaign rosters, not settlement entity lists. Train new units using weapons/money and capacity. Fleets carry chosen counts. Battles have independent 48×32 fields, automatic attacks/healing/kiting, no manual orders. Supply shortages slowly remove individual military units.
- Empty worlds are captured after travel. Colonization costs resources and six free slots, then creates a base and six new residents. Enemy victory transfers damaged buildings and part of the civilian population, preserving surviving workplace assignments. Residents repair automatically for metal.
- H6 uses exact grid-aligned footprints: 4×4 base/factory/barracks, 3×3 house/warehouse/farm/guild, 2×2 extraction/field/tower. Rendering, collision, preview and selection must agree; no sprite extends into an adjacent road. Roads have a free UI draft, atomic paid confirmation and ×2 worker speed. Local zoom 50–200% and build/optional normal grid must not affect campaign/battle coordinates. Inventories use icon/count cells.
- Use delta simulation time, never wall-clock gameplay timers. All colonies and battles tick independently of the visible screen. Roads change movement speed; A* lexicographically minimizes off-road distance first, then travel time; no corner cutting.
- Run `node --test tests/*.test.cjs`, then validate UI in a real desktop browser. Tests should cover campaign invariants and user flows rather than obsolete manual RTS commands.
- Keep README.md honest about balance and limitations. No engine migration, multiplayer or unrequested features.

- H7 second-tier production uses smaller attached workshops (weaponshop 2×2, one slot), placed against an owned operational factory (4×4, no workplaces, inventory capacity 240). Raw materials are physically carried to its inventory; recipes consume that local inventory, output enters it and supplies barracks. Default production fills storage; optional demand mode uses local stock targets. Exclusive pickup/field/gas/input-delivery claims prevent duplicate jobs. Show white/translucent workplace squares at top-left and production/storage bars below buildings.
